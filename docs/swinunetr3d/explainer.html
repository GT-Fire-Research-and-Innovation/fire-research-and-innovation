firepred · TS-SatFire next-day prediction

Will this pixel burn tomorrow?

A ground-up guide to SwinUNETR-3D, the model this repo trains: what it computes, why it was chosen, where it falls short, and what the research says to try next.

Numbers on this page come from the repository code (tools/trace_shapes.py) and from cited sources (docs/swinunetr3d/research_notes.md). Maps marked illustration are synthetic.

1 · The question

16.9 million numbers in, 65,536 yes/no answers out

Each training example is a window: six days of a fire region, with 43 measurements per pixel per day. Pick a group to see what it tells the model.

input x : 43 channels × 6 days × 256 × 256 = 16,908,288 numbers target y : 256 × 256 pixels, newly burned tomorrow (1) or not (0)
2 · The needle in the haystack

About 1 burning pixel in 447

The paper weights fire pixels 446.78 times more than background, a weight "set from the class distribution of the training set". In a window that is about 150 new-fire pixels among 65,536. Measured on 7 real training fires (84 windows): 1 pixel in 353, and 15% of windows have no new fire at all.

So accuracy is the wrong score: a model that always answers "no fire" is right more than 99.7% of the time and useless. The repo grades with F1 = 2TP / (2TP + FP + FN) and IoU = TP / (TP + FP + FN), which ignore the ocean of easy "no" answers.

3 · Attention in windows

Every token talks to its window: all 6 days, 4 × 4 patches

A token is one day of a 2 × 2-pixel patch (750 m). Full attention over all 98,304 tokens would need 9.7 billion scores per head, 38.7 GB. Swin attends only inside (6, 4, 4) windows (96 tokens, 3 km × 3 km at stage 1, every day), and every second block shifts the windows by 2 tokens so information crosses the borders. The grid below is a 16 × 16-token corner of stage 1. Tap or hover a token.

selected token tokens it attends to same window, masked (−100)

The shifted block is computed exactly like window_ops.compute_mask: the grid is rolled by 2 tokens, cut into regular windows, and token pairs that came from different regions of the roll get −100 added before the softmax (e−100 ≈ 0). A relative position bias, one learned number per head for each (Δday, Δrow, Δcol), is added to every score: 11 × 7 × 7 = 539 per head. It is attention's only sense of where and when.

4 · The staircase

Four stages zoom out from 3 km windows to 12 km tokens

After each stage, patch merging concatenates 2 × 2 neighbouring tokens and projects them to twice the channels: the map halves, each token sees more ground, and the same 4 × 4 window spans twice the distance. Time stays at 6 days throughout (the paper: "we do not use downsampling in the temporal dimension"). Select a rung.

5 · Where the weights and the work live

A transformer in name, a CNN by weight and by work

Counting SwinUNETR-3D by part (tools/trace_shapes.py) gives the surprise of the guide. The Swin transformer is about a tenth of the parameters and a tenth of the compute. The parameters sit at the bottom: the bottleneck convolution encoder10 (two 576-channel 3 × 3 × 3 convolutions on an 8 × 8 grid) holds 54% of them. The work sits at the top: the two blocks at full 256 × 256 resolution, encoder1 and decoder1, do 46% of the 161 GMAC per window, and the bottleneck only 4%.

6 · The last step

Six opinions, one forecast

The decoder climbs back to 256 × 256 with convolutions and skip connections, but it still outputs one map per input day: (B, 2, 6, 256, 256). The trainer simply averages the six (forward_model). The day-6 view, next to tomorrow, counts no more than day 1. The repo ships -m swinunetr3d_tpool, which learns the six weights and starts exactly at the plain average.

7 · The learning signal

Dice + weighted cross-entropy, and two quirks kept on purpose

Dice is a soft F1: it grades overlap, so 65,000 background pixels cannot drown out 150 fire pixels. Cross-entropy grades every pixel and gives useful gradients early. The paper trains on both, 1:1, with class weights (1, 446.78).

In MONAI's DiceCELoss the class weight also scales Dice, so at the start the loss is roughly 215 Dice + 6.5 CE. Dice also uses a sigmoid per channel while CE uses a softmax across the two channels. Both are documented in docs/KNOWN_ISSUES.md and left unchanged, because changing them changes results.

8 · Why this model, honestly

On the paper's own benchmark, the top three are tied

TS-SatFire prediction task, 6 input days, 2021 test fires (paper Table 3). The band is ±1 seed standard deviation (0.003, Table 4). U-Net-3D's lead over SwinUNETR-3D is a third of that. The paper's own diagnosis: segmentation networks are "poorly suited to inferring future states", and zeroing most weather and terrain channels barely changes F1.

9 · Shortcomings

Where the model falls short, tied to the code

The model's shape

  • The forecast is a fixed mean of six per-day maps; no layer is a "tomorrow" head.
  • Wide in time, narrow in space: stage-1 attention sees 3 km.
  • About 90% of the weights and of the compute are convolutions: parameters pile up in the 8 × 8 bottleneck, work in the two full-resolution blocks.
  • The window depends on T, so a 6-day model cannot take 5 or 8 days.

The inputs

  • Static layers (terrain, 129 TESSERA channels) are copied across all 6 days.
  • A fixed 96 km crop clips large or off-centre fires.
  • Angles keep only their sine: north and south wind look the same results
  • About 125 training fires, noisy labels, and a train/val label rule that differs between code paths.

The training signal

  • Two definitions of probability in one loss results
  • The class weight swamps Dice results
  • Checkpoints chosen by loss, scored by F1 results
  • Weight decay is logged but never applied results

Measuring and running

  • A window with no fire and no prediction scores a perfect 1.
  • One deterministic map; no uncertainty.
  • fp32 only; an unused attention mask is computed on every pass; per-GPU BatchNorm on 4 samples.

10 · Pathways

What to try next, ranked by evidence for the cost

Each pathway becomes one file in firepred/models/zoo/ (or one loss in training/losses.py): python -m firepred.cli.new_model NAME, check with python -m firepred.cli.check model -m NAME, train with -m NAME. Run three seeds before calling anything a win.
